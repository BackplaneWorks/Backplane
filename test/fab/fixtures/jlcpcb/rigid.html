FAQs about Rigid PCB Capabilities

What is the maximum number of layers supported by JLCPCB for standard production?

JLCPCB supports up to 32 layers in standard production. Layers from 1 to 6 offer the optimal balance between cost, lead time, and reliability. For designs exceeding 10 layers, we recommend submitting the stack-up diagram for engineering review prior to ordering to ensure manufacturability and controlled impedance performance.

Does JLCPCB provide impedance control, and what tolerance can be achieved?

Yes, JLCPCB offers impedance control for multi-layer boards. The standard tolerance is ±10%. Tighter tolerance of ±5% is available upon special request, which requires controlled dielectric materials, precise stack-up design, and may involve additional verification.

What are the key differences in manufacturing capabilities between standard and advanced options?

Standard options include mechanical drilling, regular trace/space, and common surface finishes with faster lead times. Advanced options such as blind/buried vias, HDI (laser vias), heavy copper (up to 3oz), and tight impedance control provide higher design density and performance but typically require DFM review and may increase both cost and production time.

Which factors most significantly affect PCB board thickness and how can it be controlled?

Board thickness is primarily determined by the number of layers, copper weight, core and prepreg thickness, and surface finish. JLCPCB offers standard thicknesses from 0.4 mm to 3.2 mm. We recommend specifying the desired finished thickness in the order notes or stack-up drawing so our engineers can optimize the build-up accordingly.

How does panelization affect pricing and manufacturing quality?

Proper panelization significantly reduces unit cost by maximizing material utilization and improving production efficiency. JLCPCB supports V-Scoring and Stamp Hole (Mouse Bite) panelization. We recommend maintaining a minimum 2 mm spacing between boards and 5–8 mm handling rails to ensure mechanical stability during assembly and shipping.
